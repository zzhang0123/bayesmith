What bayesmith does and why

bayesmith compiles a Bayesian model, written as a graph of operators, into an inference procedure that can be audited. It finds and checks the structure of the graph, solves exactly the parts that admit a closed form, hands the remainder to a numerical backend, and returns results that carry their conditions, diagnostics and provenance. It sits between the model declaration and the sampler. It is not a probabilistic programming language and not a collection of samplers.

A plan before a fit

The script below declares two small models. mixed predicts four measurements from a two-component latent x and a positive scale nu. curved passes x through an exponential and declares, wrongly, that the prediction is affine in x.

overview_plan.py
"""The plans on the Overview page. Run with: JAX_ENABLE_X64=1 python overview_plan.py"""

import jax.numpy as jnp
import numpyro.distributions as dist

import bayesmith as bs

A = jnp.array([[1.0, 0.3], [0.2, 1.0], [0.5, -0.4], [1.0, 1.0]])
DATA = jnp.array([0.4, 1.1, -0.2, 1.5])


def mixed(data):
    """Affine in x once nu is fixed; not affine in nu. Nothing is declared."""
    x = bs.sample("x", lambda: dist.Normal(jnp.zeros(2), 2.0).to_event(1))
    nu = bs.sample("nu", lambda: dist.LogNormal(0.0, 0.5))
    mu = bs.det("mu", lambda x_, nu_: (A @ x_) * jnp.exp(0.1 * nu_), x, nu)
    bs.observe("d", lambda m: dist.Normal(m, 0.5), mu, obs=data)


def curved(data):
    """Declared affine in x, but exp(A x) is not."""
    x = bs.sample("x", lambda: dist.Normal(jnp.zeros(2), 2.0).to_event(1))
    mu = bs.det("mu", lambda x_: jnp.exp(A @ x_), x, linear_in=("x",))
    bs.observe("d", lambda m: dist.Normal(m, 0.5), mu, obs=data)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    for model in (mixed, curved):
        print(f"# {model.__name__}")
        print(bs.compile(bs.trace(model, DATA)))

bayesmith.compile prints a plan before anything is sampled. Each row is a block of latents, the route chosen for it and the evidence for that choice.

In mixed, once nu is fixed the prediction is affine in x, the prior on x is Gaussian and the noise does not depend on x. The conditional posterior of x is therefore Gaussian and can be drawn exactly with one linear solve. bayesmith established this from the traced program ("structurally certified"), confirmed it numerically at three scales, and gave nu to NUTS. The execution line states the combination: NUTS moves nu, and every sweep draws x exactly given the current nu. Declaring linear_in=("x",) on mu prints the same plan; the test suite re-checks that.

In curved, the numerical check measures how far exp(A x) departs from its own linearization. The plan reports the declaration as false, with those measurements, and gives the block to NUTS. A false declaration costs speed. It does not produce a confident, wrong posterior.

Why structure should choose the inference

A graph of conditional distributions and deterministic operators fixes the joint density:

p(\theta, d) = \prod_{v \in \text{latents}} p(v \mid \mathrm{pa}(v)) \prod_{o \in \text{observed}} p(d_o \mid \mathrm{pa}(o))

Holding the other latents fixed, some blocks of that density have a known form. A block with a Gaussian prior, a prediction affine in it and noise that does not depend on it has a Gaussian conditional posterior, which one linear solve samples exactly. A positive prediction whose logarithm is affine becomes a Gaussian block after a change of variables. What remains needs a general sampler.

In common practice this choice is made by hand. Running NUTS on the whole model is correct but ignores structure that could be eliminated exactly. Hand-written conditional updates use the structure, but they are tied to the model as it was written: when the model changes, the update can become wrong without any error. bayesmith derives the choice from the graph and the task, checks every premise the choice relies on, and records the result with the conditions under which it holds.

Three premises follow.

A model is a graph of operators

trace runs an ordinary Python function once and records it as an explicit Graph. Probabilistic nodes contribute conditional densities, deterministic nodes propagate dependence, and together they are the joint distribution.

Structure chooses the inference

Within one graph, linear-Gaussian blocks take an exact solve and the rest goes to NUTS; factor_partition also solves exp-affine blocks in log space. compile writes that decision down block by block, with its evidence, before execution.

Structure is found and checked

Affine blocks are discovered from the traced program and then verified numerically. A linear_in declaration is optional: it cannot admit a block the checks reject. When a premise fails, the block goes to a general route and the plan says why.

What it does across a workflow

The design follows one model through six steps. The status column summarizes capabilities and release status.

StepQuestionEntry pointsToday
DeclareWhat joint distribution does the model state?trace, sample, det, observeImplemented
AnalyzeWhich blocks are exact? What can the data identify?identifiability, prior_sensitivityImplemented
CompileWhich route answers this question, and why?compile, compile_taskImplemented
ExecuteRun the approved plan within a budget.execute_taskExact Gaussian and NUTS posteriors, prediction and simulation implemented; residual numerical evidence not available
EvaluateIs the result reliable, and does the model fit?bayesmith.evaluationModel checks implemented; analytic evidence for whole-graph exact linear-Gaussian models
DecideWhich next step is legal and informative?none yetRoadmap

A run returns a typed Result together with the plan that produced it and a run record: seed, precision, backend, budget, termination reason and whether the answer is exact or Monte Carlo. In the task protocol, a question the available routes cannot answer returns a Refusal naming the failed premise and the measurements behind it. Faults in the model itself and failed convergence are raised as exceptions.

How it differs from the libraries it uses

Inference libraries already provide algorithms for particular structures, such as latent-Gaussian or Laplace-marginalized models. bayesmith's own job is to find and verify structure in the whole graph, combine exact and numerical routes across its parts, and state why the combination is valid for the task being asked.

What bayesmith owns

  • The Graph and its structural meaning
  • Structure checks, exact elimination and compilation of the remainder
  • A task-aware plan, and the reasons a route was chosen or refused
  • One result protocol across exact, sampling, optimization and external backends
  • Applicability, refusals, diagnostics, provenance and quality gates

What it does not do

  • Reimplement a probabilistic programming language, a distribution library or a general collection of samplers
  • Replace numerical capabilities that NumPyro, JAXNS or ArviZ already maintain
  • Become a task scheduler or an agent framework
  • Let an automated agent lower a diagnostic standard or bypass a gate
  • Change the user's scientific model by default

Structure in practice

Four worked examples show what the plan does with different amounts of structure.

One exact posterior

Three measurements of one level. The whole graph is linear-Gaussian, so the plan draws independent exact samples with no chain.

Exact groups inside NUTS

Six group means are drawn exactly given the population mean, which NUTS moves. The draws are compared with the closed-form posterior.

A hierarchical population

A population mean and eight group means, from model and simulated data to the plan (exact group means inside NUTS), recovery and chain diagnostics.

Near-Gaussian targets

One bank of exact Gaussian draws is reused for a non-Gaussian target through importance weights, and a population parameter is fitted by marginal MAP without drawing again.

The Graph chapter writes the hierarchy above as a joint density term by term. Five questions, one model runs posterior, prediction, simulation and evidence through the typed task protocol.
